<h2>What this means for preparation</h2>

<p>The content hasn't changed — students still face the same mathematics and reading. The way they take the test has. And how a student prepares needs to match how they'll actually test.</p>

<p>An adaptive, on-screen exam demands two things at once: real command of the material — deep enough that a student keeps their footing as the questions get harder — and the composure to perform on a screen, under time, with no traditional tools to lean on.</p>

<p>That second part is what most preparation misses. A student can know the material cold and still be thrown on test day by conditions they've never practiced in:</p>

<ul>

<li><strong>On-screen tools</strong> — navigating the interface instead of a pencil</li>

<li><strong>A test that adapts</strong> — each answer shaping what comes next</li>
</ul>

<p>None of this can be learned in the abstract. Unless a student practices where the environment closely mirrors test day, they meet all of it for the first time when it counts most. That is what our platform is built to reproduce.</p>

<p>Our preparation is a deliberate sequence, all on the platform we built ourselves — each stage adding what the one before it set up.</p>

<p><strong>Master the fundamentals, then exceed them.</strong> We make the fundamentals solid, then push past grade level. Because we write our own tests, we hold students to a higher standard than the exam itself requires — so they train with margin to spare, rather than meeting their ceiling for the first time when it counts.</p>

<p><strong>Onto the screen, in real conditions.</strong> Students move to full practice exams in the same on-screen format, with the same tools and the same answer-locking conditions they'll face on test day. Our goal is not merely to prepare students for one exam, but also to help them develop the academic foundation needed to succeed in a demanding high school environment.</p>

<p><strong>What is changing in 2026?</strong><br>
Beginning in fall 2026, the SHSAT moves to a computer-adaptive format, where the questions adjust to each student's performance. For the precise details of the new format, we direct families to the <a href="https://www.schools.nyc.gov/learning/testing/specialized-high-school-admissions-test" target="_blank" rel="noopener">NYC DOE's official SHSAT page</a>, which we follow closely.</p>

<p><strong>Does my child need to prepare differently than students did last year?</strong><br>
Yes. The material to master has not changed, but the experience of the test has — so preparation now has to build comfort with an adaptive, screen-based format, not only command of the content.</p>

But the new format brings constraints that last year's test didn't have: answers are locked once your child moves to the next question, sections are worked one at a time — and the test adapts as they go. <p><strong>Does your practice work exactly like the real adaptive test?</strong><br>
Not exactly — and the difference is deliberate. A test built to measure will ease off when a student stumbles. That's fine for measuring — but it's the wrong way to train, because it backs off just when a student most needs the push. Our practice gives your child the real experience — the secure setting, the pacing, answers locked once they move on — while keeping the questions hard the whole way through. A rough patch never turns into easier and easier questions. Talk to us — we're happy to walk you through how we approach it and why it makes students train harder.</p>

<p><strong>If the practice adapts to my child, will they still see difficult material?</strong><br>
Yes. The practice adjusts to each student, but the questions stay hard throughout. Your child won't end up working through questions that are too simple to teach them anything, no matter how a session unfolds.</p>

<p><strong>Where can my child practice for the adaptive SHSAT?</strong><br>
On our own adaptive practice platform, where answers lock once a student moves on — just as they will on test day. Students train on full digital exams in conditions that mirror the real test — building the rigor, the screen skills, and the composure the adaptive format asks for.</p>

<p><strong>When should we start?</strong><br>
Earlier is better. Comfort with an adaptive, screen-based format is now part of readiness itself, and students build it through steady practice over time rather than a final push.</p>
